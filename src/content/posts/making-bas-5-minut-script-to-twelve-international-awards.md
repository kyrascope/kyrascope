We shot 'बस 5 मिनट' in November 2021, over two intense days. I remember Dad being nervous in a way I'd never seen before. This wasn't a YouTube video where we could redo takes. This was it.

The concept was simple: a father and daughter sharing a meal, a conversation, five minutes of real connection. But simplicity is deceptive. Every element had to be perfect—the lighting, the sound, the performances, the editing rhythm.

Working with a professional cinematographer changed everything. Saurav didn't accept my first instinct anymore. "What are we trying to say with this shot?" he'd ask. "Is the camera position serving the story or is it a distraction?" I learned that filmmaking is about intention. Every choice matters.

Acting opposite my father was strange and beautiful. There were moments—genuine moments—where the line between "Dad" and "character" blurred. We'd do a take, and something true would happen. Other times, I'd miss the mark entirely and we'd start again. By take eight or nine, I understood what the scene needed.

The editing process took months. Our editor, Ravi, would show me rough cuts and I'd realize that moments I thought were essential could be cut without losing anything. Other scenes that seemed trivial were absolutely crucial. It taught me about the architecture of storytelling—how scenes build on each other, how rhythm carries emotion.

I watched the sound design happen from scratch. A cup placed down on a table. A breath. A pause. Silence. These tiny elements created the emotional landscape of the film. I'd never appreciated sound design before. I thought film was about what you see. It's equally about what you hear.

By early 2022, we had the final film. And then we submitted it to film festivals.

I won't lie—I was skeptical. We're a kid and her father making a film about connection and time. It's not high concept. There's no action, no twist. Just two people talking. Would anyone care?

Then the acceptances started coming in. Local festivals. National festivals. International festivals. Each screening, each award, felt surreal.

Winning twelve international festival awards wasn't something I'd ever imagined. When they started announcing our film, I'd feel this strange mix of pride and disbelief. The film had gone from a breakfast conversation to being recognized around the world.

But here's what mattered most: people responded to the emotion. Parents told us it made them cry. They said it made them want to have real conversations with their kids. Critics noted the intimacy, the specificity, the refusal to be sentimental.

We'd made something small with everything we had. And somehow, that resonated.

Looking back, I realize those two shooting days changed my trajectory. I was no longer a kid with a YouTube channel. I was a filmmaker. And that shift—from performer to storyteller—felt like growing up in the most important way.
