I was five when I first saw myself on screen. Five-year-old me was confident in a way only five-year-olds can be—no self-consciousness, just pure enthusiasm about toys.

Now I'm twelve, and I barely recognize that child.

In the early years, Kyrascope was simple. A toy appears, I react to it with genuine delight, Dad films it. That's the content. I didn't overthink it. I was too young to understand the layers—the branding, the audience, the metrics.

But as I got older, I became aware. Aware that people were watching. Aware that my opinions mattered to others. Aware that my image was being shaped by algorithms and platform logic.

By age nine, I was already thinking strategically about content. Which videos would perform better? What would get more views? I'd see myself as a brand instead of just a person trying to share something.

It wasn't all negative. That awareness made me more thoughtful. It pushed me to improve. But it also created anxiety I didn't used to have.

That's why the break from YouTube, beginning in 2020, was essential. Not because YouTube was bad. Because I needed to remember who I was without an audience watching.

When I stopped posting, something shifted. The pressure lifted. I could read a book without thinking about video ideas. I could have a bad day without worrying if it affected my "content calendar." I could just be a kid.

But stepping back also showed me something unexpected: I actually do like creating. It's not just residual momentum from being five. There's something in me that wants to make things, tell stories, communicate ideas.

The difference is intention. Toy reviews were about discovery. Filmmaking is about meaning. One was about showing people products; the other is about showing them something true about the human condition.

Physically, I've changed too. I was a small, bubbly five-year-old. Now I'm a teenager with actual opinions and a voice that's dropping slightly. My style has evolved. I'm more self-aware about how I look on camera, which brings new considerations but also new possibilities.

I've learned things no kid should have to learn. The difference between authentic engagement and bot activity. How algorithms work. How to navigate comments that are supportive and ones that are cruel. How to be a public figure while still being a person.

I've also learned gifts that only a platform can give. How to communicate clearly. How to think about craft and quality. How to connect with thousands of people across languages and geographies. How to turn ideas into reality.

Looking at myself from age five to twelve, I see a person becoming. Not a "child influencer" becoming an "adult influencer." Just a person, learning who she is in public and in private.

The early joy of unboxing toys wasn't more authentic than the current complexity of filmmaking. They're just different chapters.

I don't know who creator-Kyra will be in another seven years. Maybe I'll keep making films. Maybe I'll pivot entirely. But I'm grateful for where I've been, and I'm excited about what comes next.

The girl who started at five was purely enthusiastic. The girl at twelve is thoughtful, aware, sometimes tired.

But somewhere in between, she became someone who knows how to tell a story. And that's something worth celebrating.
