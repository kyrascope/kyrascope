December 2020. I'm sitting in our living room, looking back at the year, and it feels surreal. Nine months ago, I thought lockdown would last a few weeks. Instead, it reshaped everything.

I'd like to say I handled it gracefully. I didn't. In May, I was frustrated, lonely, and didn't understand why I couldn't see my friends or go to toy stores. I'd see other kids on Instagram and feel jealous. Why could they go out and I couldn't?

But resilience isn't about not feeling those things. It's about moving through them.

What I learned this year, more than in my entire life before, is that resilience is small. It's not dramatic. It's deciding to film a video even though you're bored of your room. It's finding joy in painting during a gray monsoon afternoon. It's reading comments from other kids in lockdown and knowing you're not alone.

I learned that my audience was more than numbers. During a global crisis, when everyone was scared, my toy reviews—something as simple as me unboxing a doll—gave people a moment of normalcy. A stressed parent watching told Dad that my video made her daughter smile when she'd been crying. That hit differently.

I learned that plans don't matter as much as flexibility. I'd dreamed of going to toy stores, traveling for brand collaborations, meeting fellow creators. None of that happened. Instead, I found creativity in limitations. I discovered video editing, painting, storytelling. I spent more time with my parents than I ever had.

I learned that social media can be dehumanizing or connecting, depending on how you use it. Instead of endlessly scrolling, I engaged. I responded to comments. I asked my audience what they wanted to see. I remembered that behind every subscriber was a real person.

As 2020 closes, I'm not grateful for the pandemic. I'm not romanticizing hardship. But I'm grateful for what I learned. Resilience isn't bouncing back to normal. It's building something new in the wreckage.

I'm nine years old. I'm still figuring out who I am. But 2020 taught me that I'm tougher than I thought.
