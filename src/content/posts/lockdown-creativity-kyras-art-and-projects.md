One unexpected gift of lockdown was time. Time I'd never had before to explore interests beyond the camera.

While I used to rush from school to shooting schedules, suddenly there were quiet afternoons where I could just... create. Not for views. Not for subscribers. Just for me.

I started with sketching. I've always loved drawing, but I was often too busy. That June, I filled entire sketchbooks with characters, scenery, and random doodles. Some were terrible—stick figures that looked more like abstract art—but others surprised me. I found I could draw intricate patterns, design my own toy characters, and lose myself for hours in colored pencil work.

Dad suggested I try acrylic painting. Our verandah became an art studio. I painted small canvases—some of my favorite toys reimagined in abstract colors, landscapes of places I missed visiting, and portraits of family members. One painting of our cat, Mittens, made Dad laugh for an hour.

But it wasn't just fine art. I got obsessed with DIY crafts. Cardboard became my best material. I made dollhouses from old boxes, designed board games from scratch, and created puppet theaters. Some were Instagram-worthy; most weren't. But each one taught me something about design, engineering, and problem-solving.

I tried photography too—macro shots of plants, shadows on walls, the way light fell through windows. I discovered that creativity wasn't limited to gadgets and toys. My phone's camera could tell stories too.

These weren't content for the channel. Most will never see an audience. But they were deeply personal. They reminded me why I started creating in the first place—not for views, but because making things brings me joy.

When schools eventually opened, I was busier than ever. But that lockdown period gave me skills and confidence I carry forward. It taught me that creative expression is its own reward.
