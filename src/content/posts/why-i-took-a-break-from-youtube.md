I've been getting DMs asking where I've been. Fair question. From 2016 to 2019, I uploaded regularly. Then 2020 happened, and uploads slowed. Now it's 2021, and I've barely posted anything.

I want to be honest about why.

At first, I thought I'd just take a small break. A month, maybe two. But as weeks turned into months, I realized something: I wasn't missing YouTube. And that was shocking to me.

For four years, making videos was my life. School, then filming. Weekends, content planning. Every interesting moment became "this would make a good video." I was ten years old and I'd already internalized the creator mindset. Part of me was always "on."

But growing up means your priorities change. As I moved from age nine to ten to eleven, I started caring more about school, making friends in real life, having experiences that weren't documented. I wanted to read books without thinking about how to turn them into content. I wanted to play without filming it.

There's a weird guilt that comes with this realization. My parents built a platform for me. My audience waited for videos. Brands wanted collaborations. And I just... needed to be a kid.

So I did something radical: I logged out. Not forever, but for a real break. I deleted YouTube from my phone. I stopped checking subscriber counts. I stopped thinking about trending topics.

Instead, I focused on school. I made friends who didn't know I was "famous"—or maybe they did, but they didn't care. I read books I actually wanted to read. I focused on being present with my family, not performing for a camera.

Here's what surprised me: I didn't miss the platform. I missed making things, yes. I missed connecting with viewers. But I didn't miss the pressure. The algorithm. The constant need to be relevant.

I'm eleven now, and I'm learning who I am when no one's watching. Turns out, I like that person too.

This isn't goodbye. But it's a reset. When I come back—and I think I will—it'll be different. More intentional. On my terms, not the algorithm's.

Thanks for being patient with me while I figure this out.
