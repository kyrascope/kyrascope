When March 2020 hit, I was nine years old, and suddenly the world I knew—going to school, making videos, visiting toy stores—came to a halt. Like millions of kids around the world, I went from the classroom to our living room turned studio.

At first, it felt like a break. No more rushing between school and filming. But I quickly realized how much I missed connecting with my audience, testing new toys, and exploring Delhi's fun places. Dad and I had to think differently about content.

We started small. Instead of elaborate outdoor shoots, we created from home. I reviewed toys I already had, did unboxing videos of things that arrived during lockdown, and experimented with DIY activities. One of my favorite memories is when we set up a mini-studio in our bedroom using natural light from the window and some cardboard backdrops.

The hardest part wasn't the lack of fancy equipment—it was missing the energy of exploring new places and meeting brands. But this period taught me something valuable: creativity isn't about having the perfect setup. It's about finding interesting angles, asking better questions, and connecting authentically with viewers.

What kept me going was reading comments from kids who said my videos helped them during quarantine. Some were stuck at home too, and watching toy reviews became a small escape. That responsibility felt real to a nine-year-old.

We weren't alone in this struggle. Creators everywhere had to pivot. Some moved to gaming, others focused on educational content. I realized the beauty of having a platform wasn't just about the glitz—it was about showing up, even when circumstances weren't ideal.

By the end of 2020, we'd uploaded just three videos, but those three felt meaningful. They were honest snapshots of what life looked like when the world paused. Looking back, I'm grateful for that unexpected lesson in resilience.
