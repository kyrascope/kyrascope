September 2023. I'm officially in Class 10—the big one. Board exams loom. Universities and post-school choices feel suddenly real instead of theoretical.

This is the year everything gets serious.

For most kids, Class 10 means intense focus on academics. For me, it's more complicated. I have a YouTube channel with subscribers. I have a film with awards. I have a website and a brand, for better or worse.

And now I have to figure out how all of that fits into a life that also requires crushing it on my boards.

I've made a decision: academics come first. Not because YouTube doesn't matter, but because education does. My parents have always told me that content comes and goes, but what you learn stays. I want to be the kind of person who takes her studies seriously, who argues a point in class, who actually understands history, science, literature.

The irony isn't lost on me. I started my channel because I was genuinely curious about products and how they worked. Over the years, that curiosity evolved into filmmaking, storytelling, trying to understand human behavior. Those are serious intellectual pursuits.

So Class 10 isn't about abandoning creativity. It's about integrating it properly. Creating intentionally, on a schedule I control, not algorithmic demands.

I'm reading more than ever. Not just for pleasure, though I'm doing plenty of that. I'm reading to understand. History, science, literature—subjects that feel abstract in textbooks come alive when you engage with them fully. I'm discovering that real learning is the best creative fuel. A history book about medieval India is more inspiring than any YouTube comment.

I'm also thinking differently about my digital footprint. Everything I've created—from toy reviews to our film—is part of my academic record now. Universities will Google me. Some will see a kid who wasted time on the internet. Others might see evidence of long-term creative commitment, self-directed learning, technical skill development.

I want to own that narrative. Not by deleting anything or being ashamed, but by continuing to create thoughtfully. By making sure that alongside my board exams, my academic achievements, my school projects, there's evidence of a person who takes her craft seriously.

The question I'm wrestling with now: Who do I want to be in five years? Not as a YouTuber or filmmaker, but as a person?

I think I want to be intelligent, thoughtful, kind. I want to understand the world. I want to make things that matter, even if they only matter to a few people. I want to not be famous, but to be competent at things I care about.

Class 10 is pushing me to think bigger than metrics and audience. It's pushing me to ask: What do I actually believe? What do I want to study in university? What kind of person do I want to become?

These questions matter more than any YouTube video.

I'm still making content. But now I'm also studying hard, reading voraciously, thinking seriously about my future. I'm a kid trying to figure out who she is, with the unusual complication that thousands of people have been watching her grow.

It's strange. But it's my life. And honestly, I'm curious to see where it goes.
